# AeroOS Project Report Sections & Content Reference

This document compiles the academic sections, rationales, and content crafted for the **AeroOS** project report (*Adaptive Telemetry-Driven Feed-Forward Micro-Ventilation for Workstation-Scale Indoor Air Monitoring*).

## 1.1 INTRODUCTION

Workstation indoor air quality plays a critical role in cognitive performance, occupant alertness, and overall physiological well-being. However, modern compact office and home workstation environments often suffer from rapid pollutant accumulation caused by metabolic carbon dioxide exhalation, volatile organic emissions from furniture and electronics, and limited passive air movement. Traditional indoor ventilation approaches predominantly depend on manual window adjustments or high-threshold reactive exhaust fans, which do not monitor micro-climatic fluctuations in real time. The Adaptive Telemetry-Driven Feed-Forward Micro-Ventilation System addresses these challenges by combining the Internet of Things, Exploratory Data Analysis, and explainable machine learning. The proposed system employs specialized environmental sensors connected to an edge microcontroller to measure localized gas levels, ambient temperature, relative humidity, and workstation computing activities. Telemetry is analyzed to understand environmental trends and generate proactive, desk-level micro-ventilation actions. An interpretable machine learning model provides clear, human-understandable rationales for every actuation, while an integrated confidence assessment routine ensures system stability and prevents false alarms. Consequently, the system maintains superior indoor air quality, suppresses threshold spikes, and promotes energy-efficient micro-ventilation through transparent, data-driven automation.

## 1.2 NEED FOR FEED-FORWARD MICRO-VENTILATION

The need for an Adaptive Telemetry-Driven Feed-Forward Micro-Ventilation System arises from the limitations of conventional reactive air exchange systems, acoustic discomfort caused by sudden high-speed fan cycling, and inefficient electrical energy consumption. Workstation occupants frequently depend on manual ventilation or reactive exhaust fans that activate only after ambient pollutant concentrations have already crossed unhealthy thresholds. Due to physical air mixing and transport lag, reactive systems permit significant pollutant overshoot before fresh air disperses, causing cognitive fatigue, headaches, and productivity decline. Continuous monitoring of air quality gas levels, ambient temperature, relative humidity, and host computing workloads allows the system to identify early micro-climatic deterioration before indoor air degrades. By integrating Internet of Things (IoT) sensors, an ESP32 microcontroller, Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA), and explainable machine learning, the proposed system provides proactive, desk-level micro-ventilation actuation accompanied by transparent rationale explanations. The system continuously evaluates prediction confidence and model uncertainty before triggering actuation. This approach enhances operational transparency, supports automated decision-making, and maintains healthy indoor breathing environments.

Effective indoor air management requires timely, preemptive actions based on actual real-time environmental trends rather than rigid schedules or delayed reactive triggers. Workstation occupants cannot manually gauge gradual volatile organic compound accumulation or subtle shifts in thermal comfort, leading to degraded air quality or unnecessary, noisy fan operation. Furthermore, existing automated ventilation units function as closed black-box mechanisms, offering no explanations for fan activations and lacking prediction confidence safeguards. To overcome these limitations, the Feed-Forward Micro-Ventilation System employs an ESP32 microcontroller interfaced with an MQ-135 air quality sensor and a DHT22 temperature-humidity sensor to sample localized telemetry alongside workstation compute activity. Exploratory Data Analysis helps uncover underlying correlation patterns between system workload and indoor pollutant buildup, while explainable machine learning generates transparent ventilation recommendations. The system evaluates prediction confidence prior to fan speed modulation, ensuring quiet, low-power micro-ventilation that suppresses air quality spikes minutes before they manifest. Therefore, the system is needed to support intelligent air management, eliminate pollutant overshoot, reduce acoustic disturbance, and deliver sustainable, energy-efficient indoor environmental comfort.

## 4.1 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The proposed Adaptive Telemetry-Driven Feed-Forward Micro-Ventilation System integrates an ESP32 microcontroller, environmental sensors, data analysis techniques, and explainable machine learning to support intelligent indoor air quality management. The system is designed to collect essential environmental and workstation parameters, including air quality gas concentration, ambient temperature, relative humidity, and host compute load, transmitting the measurements for centralized processing and analysis. The processed data is analyzed using Exploratory Data Analysis to identify environmental patterns and understand the factors influencing ventilation requirements. The explainable machine learning module generates desk-specific micro-ventilation recommendations and provides understandable plain-English explanations for its decisions. The confidence evaluation mechanism assesses prediction reliability and prevents erratic automatic actuation when confidence is insufficient. Furthermore, the adaptive decision-making module considers changing environmental trends and system workloads to adjust its recommendations dynamically. Experimental results demonstrate that the feed-forward mechanism successfully mitigates pollutant overshoot, dampens sudden acoustic fan noise, and reduces electrical energy consumption compared to traditional reactive thresholds.
